# The ten era art packs

[Compare fifty representative models](flagships.webp), inspect [fifty working facilities](working-civilization.webp), or select an era below. Each contact sheet shows six elements. For each element, columns progress from level one on the left, and rows show its four variants. Roads and bridges have as many as eleven levels. A bus stop has three.

All 61,360 catalog cells were built and rasterized through the game’s shared voxel model path. Every render has visible geometry. Within an era, the complete voxel signatures differ at every adjacent level and across all four variants of each level. The [machine-readable results](coverage-summary.json) record these checks. They measure coverage and exact duplicates. Judging visual recognition and gameplay clarity also requires the city views and human review.

The ten packs share the same 294 canonical IDs. Housing, civic halls, markets, stations and utilities use the period’s structural grammar. Boats, heavy machinery, animal pens and special-purpose structures keep their working pivots, routes and readable canonical features, with period materials, equipment and frontages. Default custom designs keep their own appearance.

## Design revisions

The [first comparison](initial-directions.webp) exposed repeated rectangular profiles and broad roof stripes. The [first revision](first-revision.webp) adds period courtyards, pylon gates, a working mill wheel, a stone loggia, signal equipment, glazed garden pods and patched cisterns. The [final comparison](flagships.webp) shows public entrances, parcel-scale trade fixtures and more exposed turf. A full street view also caught high-contrast paving seams and a modern-looking solar-panel face in the historical starter village. The final paving keeps a softer value range, and each period receives its own working solar collector. Full-catalog checks also caught duplicated adjacent shelter and stop levels. Waiting amenities and changes in roof and service levels resolve them.
