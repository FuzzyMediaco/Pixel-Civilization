# Pixel Civilization illustrated catalog redesign

Implement a visual redesign of **Original and all ten production eras** in `FuzzyMediaco/Pixel-Civilization`. Start from current `main`. I want a reason to build a whole city in each style. I want to spot the library from above, recognize the era with the HUD hidden, see what my last upgrade bought me, and zoom in to find something somebody clearly had fun making.

The Original Cheesesteak Spot is my benchmark. A little grill cart grows into rival neon storefronts and ultimately a walk-in cheesesteak. Customers order through openings in the filling. Apply that degree of invention to the catalog. Give a quiet house, a stretch of railway, and a grove of trees as much thought as a landmark. Deliver the work in the playable game and its Atlas.

## Start with what the player sees today

I reviewed `main` at `99c5b7bf61798d8630bd0747daf808f873cfd306`. Use these examples to find the corresponding paths on your working revision.

- **Original Public Library.** Its four variants share a rectangular hall and a columned porch. In the current art sheet nearly every level 4 and 5 roof reads as a flat blue rectangle. The column detail is there, but from the game camera the roof wins. The Recycling Center has a similar problem. Its ribbed shed hides the sorting equipment a player would enjoy watching. The Shoe Store’s giant shoe and the Cheesesteak Spot show what the same voxel renderer can do.
- **Five different services, one silhouette.** Put `public_library`, `post_offices`, `courthouses`, `city_offices`, and `banks` side by side at level 3 in each era. In Egypt the squat parapets dominate. In Renaissance Italy the little cupolas repeat. In 3027 the low green roof plates dominate. A player can often tell which row is which era and still struggle to tell which column is the library. Inspect the same four variants within each cell.
- **The problem extends past buildings.** The working-facilities sheet repeats a peaked farm building and bright crop patch across much of the lineup. Most aqueducts read as the same long trough on close-set piers. An Egyptian farm could be a ladder of irrigated strips, a Greek farm could follow olive terraces, and a 3027 farm could grow in suspended racks with visible roots. Give those differences enough room to show at play zoom.
- **The repetition starts in the model builders.** Original’s library in `src/game/designs/balancedExpansion.ts` begins with one hollow box. The production paths in `periodArchitecture.ts` send schools, civic halls and faith buildings through `institution()`. The library’s catalog archetype is `school_campus`. Other elements retain their Original model and receive a period finish and fixtures. `periodArtCatalog.ts` also supplies the same four variant names across most of each era. These are precise places to give individual elements their own form and names. Keep useful parts such as the Roman water engineering and the Medieval working wheel.
- **Scope.** The reviewed catalog has 294 elements across 13 categories. There are 282 five-level elements, eleven road or bridge elements with eleven levels, and one three-level Bus Stop. Four variants give 6,136 playable combinations per style and **67,496 across eleven styles**. The committed production review covers 61,360 era combinations. Its checks catch empty output and exact repeats. A one-voxel trim or a recolor can pass those checks. Recompute the playable matrix with `ELEMENT_BY_ID` and `getMaxElementLevel` on the current branch. Rome’s production style is Aqueduct City. The alternate Forum City pilot is outside the eleven-style total.

## Give Original some personality

Make the **Public Library** the first worked example. Give it four architectural ideas that a player would actually choose between. Preserve its existing footprint at every level. This is a buildable direction you can improve if you find a stronger form in the renderer.

| Level and footprint | A · Stacked Hardbacks | B · Open Folio | C · Bookend Alley | D · Pop-up Storybook |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · 1×1 | One thick book. The fore-edge opens into a tiny reading room. | Two splayed pages shelter a reading bench in the binding. | Two upright volumes frame a narrow walk-in slot. | A folded paper tree rises from a little lending kiosk. |
| 2 · 2×1 | A second offset book makes a deep porch over the return slot. | One long leaf shelters the stacks and a shorter leaf covers a street terrace. | Three uneven spines line a covered passage with a bookshop-window-sized view into the stacks. | A second fold opens a tiny storybook lane under the tree. |
| 3 · 2×2 | Three staggered books make an L around a small reading garden. | A broad V opens over sunken reading steps. A ribbon stair follows the gutter. | Two tall bookends face one another across an open reading alley. | A paper village folds up around a real walk-in courtyard. |
| 4 · 3×2 | Two stacks straddle a daylight slot. A bookmark footbridge crosses it. | Splayed page wings shelter a public reading amphitheater. | A high reading bridge connects a short row of spines to one tall archive tower. | A die-cut proscenium frames a storytelling stage between folded wings. |
| 5 · 3×3 | A terraced pile of giant hardbacks climbs to a roof garden. The ribbon stair curls out onto the street. | An enormous open atlas has two fanned roof halves, raised map relief, and a glass spine atrium. | A skyline of upright books encloses a square. You can look through a tall missing-volume gap. | An open book unfolds into a paper tree whose branches are occupied reading balconies. |

Let the pale page edges, headbands, and warm reading niches reward a close look. The front door should be obvious even if the model is frozen and every material is gray. Imagine the neighboring **Indie Bookstore** as a narrow secondhand shop with crooked bays, a street book cart and a reading loft above the till. A player should see which building lends books and which sells them.

Carry the idea beyond the library.

| Original element | A direction worth building |
| --- | --- |
| Post Office | Fold the roof like an envelope and tuck the dispatch door under the lifted flap. A later sorting hall can wrap a visible parcel belt around an open yard. Give another variant the outline of a long railway mail shed with sawtooth loading docks. |
| Recycling Center | Let a child follow one can from the drop-off bins to the sorting drum to the stacked bales. Start with exposed bins. Grow into a yard with a forked conveyor and a cutaway drum. Try a circular sorting carousel as another variant. |
| Modern Art Gallery and Jazz Club | Let visitors walk through a monumental empty picture frame into the gallery’s sculpture court. Give the club a low, dark room opening into the flared bell of a brass horn. Each should have a distinct street profile with its signs switched off. |
| Housing, farms and nature | Let a modest house wrap around a porch tree, another step down a slope, and a rowhouse frame a communal clothesline. Build a Honey & Bee Farm around clustered hive courts and flowering flight paths. At the Tide Pool Walk, the shapes of the pools and the path between them are the attraction. |
| Transit | A Train Stop announces the **boarding edge** with shelter, steps and waiting space. A Train Depot announces the **engine entrance** with inspection pits, sheds and eventually a fan of service bays. Follow the real rails through both. Make them easy to tell apart in a busy yard. |

Balance the city. Modest buildings earn character through proportion, garden walls, roof openings and how they meet the street. Save the biggest visual jokes, motion and skyline breaks for places that deserve attention. A block where every roof is shouting is as hard to read as a block of plain boxes.

## Art direction for all eleven styles

Use the library as a controlled comparison. Then give every other element its own idea at the same level of specificity. The names below are proposed art directions. Take liberties with history whenever they make the city more beautiful or communicate the element’s job.

| Style | A library you could recognize from above | The ordinary street and a working building |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Original** | A book-built neighborhood landmark. Choose between a horizontal stack, an open V, upright spines, or folded paper. | A porch house and a quiet garden make room for the hoagie counter at the corner. Across the street, the recycling drum exposes the path from cans to bales. |
| **Ancient China** · `ancient-china` | **Bamboo Slip Archive.** Hinged strips fan from a binding court. One curves into a covered reading walk over a garden. | Garden walls open into offset courts. A grocer spreads produce under folding silk awnings. A timber lifting wheel feeds a stepped water channel. Reserve the tallest layered eaves for the skyline. |
| **Ancient Egypt** · `ancient-egypt` | **House of Unrolled Papyrus.** Two rolled roof ends shade a long reading terrace. Bundle-shaped columns cast deep stripes across it. | Low homes share linen shade. Round grain silos rise at the market. A counterweighted lifting beam tips water into descending filter beds. A neighborhood should read as Egypt even with the nearest pyramid out of frame. |
| **Ancient Rome · Aqueduct City** · `rome-aqueduct` | **Vaults of the Record Keepers.** Thick brick barrel vaults open toward a stepped reading court. Scroll niches cut deep into the ends. | Dense insulae cast street shadows. A horseshoe food market encloses an open trading floor. A raised channel pours into bathhouse settling pools. Give arches different spans and jobs. |
| **Ancient Greece** · `ancient-greece` | **Olive Grove Lyceum.** A semicircle of outdoor reading steps faces an olive tree. A narrow painted stoa shelters the scroll collection along one edge. | Stagger pale hillside terraces around open air. Set market tables under separate sailcloth triangles. Let water descend through carved bowls in an exposed spring court. |
| **Medieval Europe** · `medieval-europe` | **Lantern Scriptorium.** A tall timber reading lantern leans over a low copying wing. An exterior stair wraps the book court below. | Crooked gables overhang the lane. A baker’s oven protrudes into a side yard. The waterworks straddles its wheel and race. Let worn timber, quilted fields and slate roofs carry the quieter blocks. |
| **Renaissance Italy** · `renaissance-italy` | **Printer’s Piazza.** A giant timber screw press spans two loggias over a print court. Fresh sheets hang in its inner light well. | Palazzo fronts open onto measured squares. A market bridge displays separate craft bays. In the waterworks, a carved cascade descends into a symmetrical pair of settling courts. Give the skyline’s dome breathing room. |
| **World War II era** · `world-war-ii` | **Lending Carriage Hall.** Retired railway reading cars flank a platform. A glazed footbridge later joins them into a neighborhood library. | Keep Allied and Axis regional influences in one civilian era. Pair brick terraces and allotments with a ration cooperative, barrel-roof stores and a sawtooth rail works. Let blackout shades and platform lamps shape the evening street. |
| **Art Deco metropolis** · `art-deco` | **Fanlight Library.** Broad fan-shaped reading rooms spread from a low lobby. Two fluted bookends frame an amber clerestory. | Give a corner grocer a streamlined prow. Make an express station low and long with a ribbed canopy. Reserve the tallest setbacks for civic and commercial towers. Brass catches daylight as well as marquee light. |
| **3027** · `future-3027` | **Memory Orbit.** Reading pods occupy a broken ring held on three ribs. Look through the center to a planted ground entrance. | Homes perch in branching clusters. A food exchange docks deliveries at the bite in a crescent. The waterworks has translucent filter gills. Make the gaps, supports and planted undersides visible in daylight. |
| **Post-apocalyptic** · `post-apocalyptic` | **Reservoir of Stories.** A salvaged cistern is sliced open to reveal curved shelves and a greenhouse reading deck inside the old rim. | Houses grow around repaired frames. Patchwork sails shade a barter yard. A rainworks routes mismatched gutters into reclaimed tanks. Later neighborhoods show trees, repaired machinery and places people are proud to inhabit. |

Translate the governing idea as well as the materials. For example, take the Roman grocer’s customer flow around a court, the Greek grocer’s open slope and the futuristic grocer’s docking crescent. These should give three genuinely different overhead plans for the same gameplay function. Put an ordinary house and its neighboring business beside each one before judging the result.

## Build the whole catalog

1. **Inventory the playable combinations.** Produce a worklist keyed by style and canonical ID, with supported levels, all four variant slots, current builder and source of labels. Record the nearest lookalike, the feature visible from above, what changes at each level, and the four architectural choices. Mark strong existing designs with a visible reason to keep them. Include all 13 categories, especially the dense civic, commercial, public safety and housing families, along with farms, networks, vessels, terrain, faith buildings and named landmarks.
2. **Build one difficult neighborhood first.** Put the five civic lookalikes, a home, food shop, plant, farm, train stop and depot on the same saved layout. Build the Original library’s full 5×4 matrix. Give every era its own library and working street. Revise the first comparison wherever two services still look interchangeable, then carry the working approach through the rest of the catalog in this assignment.
3. **Implement at the shared art boundary.** Trace `src/game/voxelArchitect.ts`, the Original builders under `src/game/designs/`, `periodArchitecture.ts`, `periodArtCatalog.ts`, `periods/pack-*.ts`, and the Roman override in `romePilot.ts`. Resolve a deliberate design per era and element. Reuse masonry, plants, rollers, arches, lights and other small parts. Give the element control over footprint composition, roofline, openings and service side. A retained Original machine can keep its moving pivot while its era gets a genuinely different surrounding facility. Keep lazy loading, cache identity and deterministic variants working.
4. **Write a real upgrade and variant story.** At any fixed variant, every playable level should reveal a new structural or operational idea. At any fixed level, all four variants should be choices someone might build for different neighborhoods. Change the roof outline, orientation of wings within the parcel, open space, equipment arrangement, or street relationship. Color and signs can reinforce a choice once its geometry reads. For a constrained road join, put the distinction in its permitted edges, supports, verge treatment and marking rhythm. Preserve the traveled surface and sightlines. For a tree or inlet, work with branching, canopy gaps, bank contour and habitat.
5. **Make the work and the details visible.** For example, grow an Egyptian water treatment plant from a single filter jar and basin, to paired settling courts, to a branched reed bed, to a raised channel feeding several beds, to a stepped water garden with an exposed clean outlet. Try a long serpentine run, paired round courts, radial beds or a horseshoe plan for its four variants. At city zoom, show the water path. At normal zoom, show the outlet and service entrance. Up close, let a wet mark appear when water returns. Use real simulation state for service cues. Decorative motions can be quiet and occasional. Make reduced-motion and static views equally readable.
6. **Put the same artwork in the player’s hands everywhere.** Update element, level and variant display names, growth text, and search aliases where the art changes. Trace `src/game/eraStyles.ts` for era naming and `src/game/designStyles.json` for Original variant labels. Check in-world models, Build icons, Details, the Atlas, its fallback images and the illustrated Handbook. Regenerate affected baked sheets and thumbnails, including Original via `scripts/render-handbook.mjs`. Opening the same era, ID, level and variant on any surface should show the same design. Preserve IDs, saved variant selections, custom art, footprints, placement and picking, network joins, costs, progression and simulation behavior. Keep multiplayer paused.
7. **Judge the rendered city.** Use the same camera, plot layout, stage and daylight for before and after. Include a starter street, a repeated residential block, the five civic buildings, a working district, a crossing, and a mature skyline. Compare all eleven with the HUD hidden. Also show a landmark-free street, a grayscale overhead view, a flat silhouette, normal phone play zoom and close detail. Shuffle unlabeled shots and check whether people can identify the era, locate the library/post office/bank, order adjacent upgrades and distinguish variants. Reuse the repo’s target of four of five reviewers identifying the era per view, and four of five functions recognized. Record genuine responses separately from automated or solo visual checks. Fix confusing models and recapture them from the same angle.
8. **Verify and hand back the result.** Extend the full-catalog art check to cover Original alongside production packs. Use exact duplicate checks plus near-silhouette comparisons to flag candidates for visual review. Verify model and fallback parity, joined roads and bridges, train boarding and depot access, actual water and delivery states, save round trips, and performance on the same dense-city camera path before and after. Run the existing art, Handbook and simulation tests and a production build. Deliver a reviewable change, playable preview when available, the coverage totals, and direct links to the before/after city boards and selected full level/variant sheets. Identify any physical-device or independent human checks still outstanding.
